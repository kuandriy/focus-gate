## Chapter 1 — 2026-05-07 — Initial bootstrap
**Time marker:** 2026-04-22..2026-05-07

### What
Focus Gate's design rests on a four-stage refinement pipeline that turns transient
prompts into durable shared memory: (1) sliding-window forest pruning drops one-offs
while reinforcing repeated topics; (2) crystallization turns surviving candidates
into append-only stories with per-memory indexes; (3) local LLM-mediated review
decides append vs. create vs. discard against existing personal memories; (4) PR
review against a shared repo collapses overlaps so frequency reweights what surfaces.
Each stage cuts input volume by an order of magnitude; what survives all four is
"knowledge worth keeping" by construction.

### Why
The premise is that the user is the only honest signal of what matters. Prescriptive
systems (skills, hand-curated docs) drift from reality the moment the codebase
changes; nobody pays the cost of keeping them in sync. Session recorders capture
everything and bury the signal. By treating the user's attention as the unit of
memory and refining it stage-by-stage, the system grows with experience but stays
manageable in size and ranking — even after hundreds of sessions, even across years,
even across a team. Frequency is the cornerstone signal: the topics you keep coming
back to are the ones that turned out to matter.
